Principles

What I believe about design

01

Research before pixels, always

I've never regretted spending more time in research. I've often regretted spending too little. The most expensive thing in design is building the wrong thing beautifully.

02

Great UX disappears

The best interfaces are the ones people don't notice. They just accomplish what they came to do and leave. Invisibility is the highest compliment a designer can receive.

03

Complexity is not the user's problem

In 17 years of enterprise design, I've never met a user who wanted complexity. Complexity is always our problem — a systems problem, an organisational problem, a design problem. The user just wants to get things done.

04

Design influence grows through business alignment

The designers who get a seat at the table are the ones who connect design decisions to business outcomes. I've spent 17 years learning to speak both languages fluently.

05

AI should make humans feel more capable

Not replaced. Not dependent. Not overwhelmed. Capable. That's the north star I use for every AI experience I design — and it's harder to achieve than it sounds.

06

Mentoring is design work too

Every designer I've helped grow makes the work better — not just theirs, but mine. Sharing craft, perspective, and feedback is how design culture compounds. It's the highest-leverage work I do.

Outside the work

The long game

Cricket — the long game

I'm a passionate cricket fan, and I've always seen parallels between cricket and design. T20s are design thinking workshops — fast, constrained, high energy. One-Day matches are product discoveries and MVPs — build something real, learn from it. Test matches are long-term design programmes — patience, skill, and resilience tested over five days.

I see myself as a Test player: built for the long game, where maturity and method matter more than speed.

Portraits in pencil and colour

I draw portraits — in pencil, poster colours, and mixed media. It's the part of my week with no briefs, no stakeholders, and no design system. Just observation, patience, and a blank page. I think it keeps my eye sharp for what makes a face — or an interface — feel right.

Photography

Seeing what others miss

I shoot with a Nikon D5300 — mostly travel and moments with family and friends. Photography taught me something design school didn't: the best composition is often about what you leave out, not what you put in.
